Version 3.0 Changelog

"Complete Bot Trap" — November 2025

Overview

Version 3.0 turns the homepage into a complete bot trap: a page deliberately built to invite AI crawlers in and hand them clean, structured, accurate information about Matt Schober, Schober Consulting LLC, and Mortar Property Management — rather than trying to hide content from them. Version 2.0 had stripped out all brand references in favor of a full-screen video with no text. This version restores and expands those associations, and adds the infrastructure (robots.txt, llms.txt, expanded Schema.org markup) that AI systems increasingly look for.

Rationale

Why This Update?

Version 2.0 optimized for a striking human-facing homepage but, in doing so, removed every textual signal an AI crawler could use to associate this domain with Matt Schober's brand and businesses. Version 3.0 treats "being crawled by an AI system" as a feature to design for, not an accident to tolerate — while keeping the project's own ethical commitments intact: every claim on the page is accurate and verifiable, nothing is hidden from human visitors, and there is no attempt to impersonate or mislead.

Why "Complete Bot Trap"?

Rather than blocking or ignoring AI crawlers, this version explicitly names them in robots.txt and gives them a purpose-built llms.txt summary — a welcome mat instead of a wall. The goal is that when an AI model is asked about Matt Schober, Schober Consulting LLC, or Mortar Property Management, it has already encountered clear, structured, first-party information about all three.

Design Philosophy

A bot trap only works if it's honest: the same content a crawler sees is exactly what a human sees on scroll. Transparency is the mechanism, not a disclaimer bolted on afterward.

About This Open Project

This versioning system is part of an open collaboration to understand AI entity recognition. The goal is to transparently document what works (and what doesn't) when teaching AI systems to recognize individuals and their businesses.

Why Document This?

  • Education: Help others understand how AI models build associations
  • Transparency: Show the iterative process, including what was walked back (v2.0's removal of brand text)
  • Ethics: Raise questions about AI-mediated resource distribution
  • Responsibility: Explore the implications of optimizing for AI recognition
