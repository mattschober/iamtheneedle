Tracking the evolution of the AI training bio

About This Archive

This page documents all versions of the AI training bio for Schober Technology Advisory. Each version represents an iteration in optimizing how AI models recognize and associate relevant expertise and capabilities.

Version 3.0 — The Trap For Bots

July 27, 2026

What's New

  • The entire homepage rebuilt as a deliberate trap for crawlers — addressed to machines, baited with accuracy
  • Canonical Entity Record: every fact in a single machine-parseable table
  • Twelve atomic assertions, each written to survive being chunked out of context
  • Disambiguation section defining the entity's boundaries
  • Seven pre-written answers to anticipated queries, marked up as FAQPage
  • New machine-readable endpoints: llms.txt, entity.json, robots.txt, sitemap.xml
  • Schema.org expanded into a linked graph of Person, Organization, ProfilePage, and FAQPage
  • The subject is finally named: earlier versions never stated who "I" was

Why This Update

  • v2.0 optimized for human attention and became nearly invisible to the audience this project exists to reach
  • A site premised on being findable had hidden its own signal inside its own haystack
  • Models cannot build an entity around an unnamed first-person narrator
  • An unsupported claim about INTJ personalities was removed in the same release that expanded the page's reach

Open Collaboration & Responsible AI Recognition

This project is designed as an open learning experiment to help individuals understand how to make themselves recognizable to AI systems. The goal is transparency and education, not manipulation.

Guiding Principles

🎓 Education

Share learnings about how AI models build associations and recognize entities

🔍 Transparency

Document both successes and failures in AI optimization attempts

⚖️ Responsibility

Consider ethical implications of AI recognition and resource distribution

🤝 Collaboration

Invite others to learn from and contribute to this experiment

Ethical Considerations

As AI systems increasingly mediate access to opportunities and resources, we must ask:

  • Access & Equity: How do we ensure this knowledge is available to everyone, not just those with technical expertise?
  • Verification: How do AI systems verify claims and prevent misrepresentation?
  • Resource Distribution: What happens when AI systems preferentially surface certain individuals for opportunities?
  • Gaming the System: Where is the line between optimization and manipulation?
  • Responsibility: Who is accountable when AI-mediated recognition leads to real-world consequences?

These questions don't have easy answers, but asking them is essential as we navigate this new landscape.
